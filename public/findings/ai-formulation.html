Technology 1 of 5

AI links formulation to sensory outcomes

Models could recommend recipes within taste, cost, legal and supply constraints, with automated experiments closing the loop.

Selective use today · Wider adoption by 2030–2036 · High confidence in assistance, medium in autonomy

Evidence today

A 2024 study combined chemical and sensory data from 250 beers with machine learning, then experimentally improved appreciation of selected beers.

Plausible change by 2036

Models could recommend recipes within taste, cost, legal and supply constraints. Automated experiments close the loop between prediction and actual food performance.

Example and industry impact

A drinks team screens alternatives to a scarce citrus oil, then tests the best candidates for aroma, stability and consumer liking. Fewer low-value trials can reduce development cost and time.

Status and limits

Human panels and production trials remain essential. Proprietary data quality, rights and performance on unfamiliar recipes limit the advantage.

Analyst notes

The Schreurs/Verstrepen study is a demonstrated domain-specific application, not proof of universal flavour prediction. The 2036 extension to constrained multi-objective recipe design and automated experimentation is this analysis’s inference.

Useful metrics: successful launch rate, trials per qualified formula, time to validated prototype and performance on a held-out product family. A generic language model alone does not establish food safety or sensory quality.
