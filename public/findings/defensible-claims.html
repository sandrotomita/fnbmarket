Regulation 5 of 5

Claims need more defensible evidence

“Natural”, “upcycled” and environmental positioning may need more precise, product-specific support.

2026–2036 · High confidence in scrutiny, medium on additional future rules

Evidence today

EU consumer rules against misleading environmental claims apply from September 2026. Existing flavour law also limits when a named source can accompany “natural”.

Plausible change by 2036

“Natural”, “upcycled” and environmental positioning may need more precise, product-specific support. Marketing teams work with ingredient and regulatory teams earlier.

Example and industry impact

A brand using fermentation-derived vanillin checks the legal flavour designation instead of assuming “natural vanilla”. A lower-impact ingredient claim needs evidence relevant to the actual product and supply chain.

Status and limits

Environmental-claims rules and named-source rules already exist. Their wider commercial influence is the forecast.

Analyst notes

Directive 2024/825 is distinct from the separate Green Claims proposal; the latter is not enacted.

EU Article 16 requires at least 95% by weight of the flavouring component to come from the named source for the specific natural named-source designation, with other conditions and alternative permitted descriptions. “Natural flavouring” and “natural vanilla flavouring” are not interchangeable.

Biotechnology does not automatically determine natural status; process, source and jurisdiction matter. Claims substantiation is separate from ingredient market authorisation.
