Technology 3 of 5

Enzymes change ingredients inside the food

More manufacturers could tailor sweetness, texture and functionality during processing; suppliers sell a process recipe with the enzyme.

Commercial foundations today · 2026–2032 expansion · High confidence in incremental adoption

Evidence today

IFF’s NURICA demonstrates in-situ conversion of lactose-derived sugars into galactooligosaccharides, rather than only adding a separate fibre ingredient.

Plausible change by 2036

More manufacturers could tailor sweetness, texture and functionality during processing. Ingredient suppliers increasingly sell a process recipe with the enzyme.

Example and industry impact

A dairy plant develops a reduced-sugar yoghurt using controlled enzyme treatment, then adjusts culture and flavour. The opportunity is lower formulation complexity and a differentiated nutritional profile.

Status and limits

Time, temperature, side reactions and enzyme inactivation affect output. Fibre recognition and label claims need market-specific verification.

Analyst notes

This differs from precision fermentation: the value arises by converting the food matrix during processing rather than manufacturing and purifying a target molecule in a separate microbial process. NURICA is a supplier-provided commercial example.

Claimed sugar reduction and fibre content vary with substrate, recipe and process, so no universal percentage is assumed. Changes in sweetness and fermentation kinetics require sensory and production validation.
