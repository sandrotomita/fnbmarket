Fermentation as an ingredient factory

High-value sweeteners, flavour molecules and selected functional proteins are plausible early winners; bulk replacement remains conditional on process economics.

Commercial in selected ingredients · 2028–2036 expansion · Medium confidence in broad scale-up

Evidence today

EverSweet is a commercial example of fermentation-derived Reb M and Reb D sweeteners. Public techno-economic work identifies major cost and scale constraints.

Plausible change by 2036

High-value sweeteners, flavour molecules and selected functional proteins are plausible early winners. Bulk replacement remains conditional on process economics.

Example and industry impact

A supplier offers an approved fermentation-derived aroma molecule as an alternative sourcing route. A brand qualifies it for taste and legal designation, then contracts for dependable capacity.

Status and limits

Cost depends on yield, productivity, feedstock, purification and plant utilisation. Fermentation does not automatically mean lower emissions or “natural”.

Analyst notes

Supplier material verifies EverSweet as an existing example, not a universal cost or sustainability advantage. The GFI/Hawkwood report is an original sector analysis with an alternative-protein advocacy perspective. Selective expansion is a scenario.

Compare total delivered cost-in-use rather than price per kilogram alone, including dose, function, downstream recovery and quality. For dairy-identical proteins, an animal-free production route must not be treated as evidence of absence of milk allergens.
