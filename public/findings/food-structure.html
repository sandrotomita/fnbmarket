Food structure controls texture and flavour

Microgels, emulsions and encapsulation could recover creaminess with less fat, control flavour release or protect sensitive aromas.

Established platforms, emerging applications · 2028–2036 · Medium confidence in adoption breadth

Evidence today

Research by Sarkar and colleagues shows plant-protein microgels can improve lubrication. McClements’ work connects colloidal structure with texture and delivery.

Plausible change by 2036

Microgels, emulsions and encapsulation could recover creaminess or control flavour release with less fat, or protect sensitive aromas in difficult recipes.

Example and industry impact

A sauce developer combines protein microgels with an aroma system to test a creamy, lower-fat product. A beverage team protects citrus aroma through processing and storage.

Status and limits

Bench-scale lubrication is not proof of consumer acceptance. Heat, acidity, shear, shelf life and regulatory classification determine viable applications.

Analyst notes

Kew et al. demonstrated microgel lubrication in experimental systems. The commercial product applications here are hypothetical development briefs, not claims that identical products exist or that sensory equivalence is proven.

Microencapsulation and emulsions already exist; the disruption is broader precision in joint texture and flavour design. Not every microstructure is an engineered nanomaterial, but relevant novel-food/nanomaterial rules must be evaluated when applicable.
