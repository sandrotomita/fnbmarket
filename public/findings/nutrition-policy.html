Regulation 2 of 5

Nutrition policy reshapes recipes

Taxes, nutrient-based rules and procurement standards could make sugar and sodium reduction an operating requirement in more markets.

2026–2036 · High confidence for sugar reformulation, medium for wider policy expansion

Evidence today

The UK announced a lower soft-drink levy threshold of 4.5 g sugar/100 ml and expansion to certain milk and plant-based drinks from January 2028.

Plausible change by 2036

Further taxes, nutrient-based rules and procurement standards could make sugar and sodium reduction an operating requirement in more markets.

Example and industry impact

A beverage brand replaces part of its sugar with an approved sweetener, then adjusts aroma, acidity and body. Food producers face similar taste challenges when reducing sodium. Integrated systems gain over standalone sweeteners.

Status and limits

UK policy decision with legislative implementation steps. A universal ultra-processed-food tax is not assumed.

Analyst notes

The UK final policy decision changed the proposed 4 g threshold to 4.5 g. Scope includes pre-packaged milk-based drinks with added sugar, with a lactose allowance, and plant-based drinks with added sugar. Exemptions and sugar-accounting rules matter. A technical legislative process was specified before January 2028 implementation.

Forecast expansion to other jurisdictions is an inference, not enacted global policy. Sugar removal can affect preservation and physical performance as well as taste, so recipes require validation.
