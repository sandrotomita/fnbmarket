Regulation 4 of 5

Origin evidence becomes a condition of supply

Supplier origin evidence could become a routine buying requirement well beyond the strictly regulated products.

2026–2036 · High confidence for covered EU products, medium for broader expansion

Evidence today

EUDR covers specified commodities and listed derived products, including relevant cocoa, coffee, soy and palm-oil products. Main application dates are December 2026 and June 2027.

Plausible change by 2036

Supplier evidence could become a routine buying requirement well beyond the strictly regulated products. The ability to preserve traceability through blending gains value.

Example and industry impact

A chocolate manufacturer qualifies cocoa batches with the required origin evidence and builds backup supply. Ingredient distributors invest in chain-of-custody data. Small suppliers need shared systems to remain competitive.

Status and limits

Enacted, with phased dates and role-specific duties. There is no assumed universal ingredient passport or global carbon label.

Analyst notes

Latest Commission material reflects the 2025 postponement and simplification. Dates and obligations depend on actor, size and product, and newly added products have later dates. Do not imply that all palm derivatives or every flavour ingredient fall within EUDR.

The 2036 data package discussed here is a commercial scenario, not an existing legal digital passport requirement. Data verification and supplier inclusion are material costs.
