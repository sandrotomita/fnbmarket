Food ingredients in 2036 · Ten-year strategic horizon

Suppliers will increasingly sell a verified product outcome, alongside the ingredient.

A market vision with flavours at its centre: five regulatory disruptions and five technology disruptions that will reshape food and beverage ingredients over the next decade.

A typical buying brief in 2036

“Keep our familiar taste. Lower sugar. Work with two protein sources. Show safety, legal status and origin for every market.”

Where value is likely to move

Value moves towards integrated flavour, texture and nutrition systems, supported by application data and regulatory evidence. Commodity ingredients remain the volume backbone.

Central scenario: stricter evidence requirements, uneven market access and selective technology scale-up. This is a strategic hypothesis, not a market-size forecast.

The strategic choice

Build the capability to reformulate without losing the consumer.

Commit now

Sensory data, sound ingredient dossiers, qualified replacement recipes and application teams that work with manufacturing.

Scale only with evidence

Fermentation capacity, specialised delivery systems and side-stream processing that meet a real customer’s cost and performance requirements.

The likely 2036 advantage: better taste under tighter constraints, delivered reliably and supported by evidence.

Scope: food and beverage ingredients, including flavours, sweeteners, proteins, fats, fibres, enzymes, colours and stabilisers. The lens is global, illustrated by the EU, US, UK and Singapore.
