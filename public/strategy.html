Strategy

A staged response through 2036

Backcasting from the central scenario. Proposed management gates, not industry benchmarks.

2026–2028

Map exposure and claims. Qualify priority replacements.

Evidence before expansion: Legal pathway, owner and fallback for each ingredient.

2028–2031

Pilot AI, enzymes and texture systems in customer products.

Evidence before expansion: Consumer liking, shelf life and positive cost-in-use case.

2031–2033

Scale qualified biomanufacturing and side-stream partnerships.

Evidence before expansion: Repeatable commercial batches. Contracted demand supports cost.

2033–2036

Expand successful systems. Maintain alternative recipes.

Evidence before expansion: Margin, resilient supply and consumer preference.

Backcasting from the central scenario: proposed management gates, not industry benchmarks. R&D, regulatory affairs, procurement and marketing need a shared recipe-and-evidence record with controlled access. Do not buy a large fermentation plant solely because a molecule works in the lab. Set capital gates around customer-qualified batches, downstream recovery, audited full costs and demand commitments. Review the scenario at least annually and after major rule changes.

The strategic choice

Build the capability to reformulate without losing the consumer.

Commit now

Sensory data, sound ingredient dossiers, qualified replacement recipes and application teams that work with manufacturing.

Scale only with evidence

Fermentation capacity, specialised delivery systems and side-stream processing that meet a real customer’s cost and performance requirements.

The likely 2036 advantage: better taste under tighter constraints, delivered reliably and supported by evidence.

Final synthesis is analyst judgment. Rankings reflect expected breadth of exposure, magnitude of recipe or cost disruption and persistence through 2036. They are ordinal strategic priorities, not quantified probabilities. No expert or supplier named on this site endorses these forecasts.
